    Concept of the day

    Why interest rates move stocks
    A stock is worth today's value of the cash it will earn in the future. Higher interest rates make future cash worth less today and make safe bonds more attractive, so stocks — especially fast-growing ones whose profits lie far ahead — tend to fall when rates rise, and rise when cuts are expected. Markets move on the surprise: a cut everyone expected is often already in the price.
